Insulin resistance (IR) is the primary metabolic driver in up to 75–85% of women with PCOS, and occurs with higher severity in South Asian populations due to the "Asian Indian Phenotype" (higher visceral fat and central adiposity at normal or low BMI).

### Clinical Mechanism
When target tissues (muscle, adipose) become resistant to insulin, the pancreas compensates by secreting higher levels of insulin (hyperinsulinemia). Excess circulating insulin acts directly on the ovarian theca cells in synergy with luteinizing hormone (LH) to overstimulate androgen production, while suppressing sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) synthesis in the liver. This cascade leads to elevated free testosterone.

### Laboratory Assessment: HOMA-IR
Standard fasting glucose alone frequently misses early insulin resistance. The Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA-IR) evaluates both fasting glucose and fasting insulin:
$$\text{HOMA-IR} = \frac{\text{Fasting Glucose (mg/dL)} \times \text{Fasting Serum Insulin (\mu\text{IU/mL})}}{405}$$

- **Optimal Insulin Sensitivity:** $\text{HOMA-IR} < 1.9$
- **Early Insulin Resistance:** $\text{HOMA-IR } 1.9 - 2.5$
- **Significant Insulin Resistance:** $\text{HOMA-IR} > 2.5$

Lifestyle modifications targeting insulin sensitivity—such as protein-first meals, complex fiber, and post-meal walking—are the first-line therapeutic foundations.
