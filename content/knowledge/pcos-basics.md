Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a complex neuroendocrine and metabolic disorder affecting approximately 15–20% of Indian women of reproductive age. It is characterized by hormonal imbalances, ovulatory irregularity, and frequently elevated ovarian androgen production.

Under the international **Rotterdam Criteria (2003/2023)**, diagnosis requires meeting at least two of the following three features:
1. **Ovulatory Dysfunction (OD):** Infrequent (oligomenorrhea) or absent (amenorrhea) menstrual cycles.
2. **Hyperandrogenism (HA):** Clinical signs (hirsutism, acne, hair thinning) or biochemical blood evidence (elevated total/free testosterone, DHEAS).
3. **Polycystic Ovarian Morphology (PCOM):** Ultrasound showing $\ge 20$ follicles per ovary or an elevated anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) level.

Based on these combinations, patients fall into four distinct phenotypes:
- **Phenotype A (Full/Classic):** OD + HA + PCOM. Highest risk for insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome.
- **Phenotype B (Non-PCO Classic):** OD + HA without ultrasound morphology.
- **Phenotype C (Ovulatory):** HA + PCOM with regular menstrual cycles.
- **Phenotype D (Non-Hyperandrogenic):** OD + PCOM with normal androgen levels.

Because every phenotype expresses different physiological drivers, interventions must be personalized through structured N-of-1 observation rather than generalized advice.
